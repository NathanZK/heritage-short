**"The Tree That Was Always There": no real likeness, so no consent record.** The story is the filmmaker's own
childhood memory, narrated in the first person. No likeness of a real person is used:

- The three children on screen are fictional representations, not images of the filmmaker, his sister or his cousin.
  Their reference image was generated by the filmmaker with Nano, and every keyframe was generated by FLUX.2 [klein]
  4B (`EXPERIMENTS.md` E9). No real family member's face was used as a likeness.
- No real person's voice is cloned or imitated: the narration uses Gemini TTS's prebuilt voice `Algieba`.
- The aunt, the sister and the cousin are mentioned in the narration but never shown or voiced as themselves.

No consent was sought, because none is needed for this; the table above stays empty rather than recording consent
that was not given.

## Data

Free-tier Gemini prompts and outputs may be used by Google to improve its products, and human reviewers may read
them (Google's terms, read 2026-11-25). Keep private family details out of text sent to Gemini unless you use a paid key.
Keyframes and prompts uploaded to Kaggle go in private datasets; confirm they are private.
`runs.jsonl` is tracked (it is assessment evidence) and records your narration text and prompts: review
`git diff runs.jsonl` for sensitive content before every commit, and before pushing to a public repository.
`outputs/` holds your media and stays git-ignored, so it cannot reach a public repository by accident; decide
deliberately what to publish with `git add -f outputs/<path>`.
